A historian with 70,000 points becomes usable when people can start with a piece of equipment and ask what it measures. In AVEVA PI, a mill can expose a named attribute such as feed rate that resolves to its local historian point. Seeq lets a user search within that mill’s branch of an asset tree. Cognite lets software follow a link from the equipment to its time series. Plants also curate a smaller set of measurements and calculated KPIs for questions operators ask repeatedly. [Cameron LNG case study](https://www.aveva.com/content/dam/aveva/documents/perspectives/success-stories/SuccessStory_AVEVA_CameronLNG.pdf) · [Barrick metallurgy case study](https://cdn.osisoft.com/osi/presentations/2018-uc-san-francisco/UC18NA-D2MI06-TRana-BarrickGold-Metallurgy-Analytics-_Transforming-Plant-Data-Actionable-Insights.pdf)

**For a small on-site agent, I would make search return candidates and make the historian read require a confirmed point ID.** For “SAG mill 2 feed rate,” search within the mill first. Use exact tag codes and approved aliases when available, then search descriptions by meaning. Return the asset path, units and whether each candidate is a measurement, setpoint or calculation. If two belt scales still qualify, show both. Give the read tool access only to approved IDs and time ranges. I found published tests of the individual steps, but no public benchmark in the sources reviewed that establishes how often an on-site LLM selects the exact intended point from a mine historian with tens of thousands of tags. [Oilfield tag-normalization workflow](https://jpt.spe.org/proposed-framework-normalizes-completions-tags) · [Small-model study at a production plant](https://doi.org/10.3389/frai.2026.1764367)

## What tag-search and tag-mapping evaluations actually measured

| Source and publication date | Method, result and limits |
|---|---|
| **Proposed Framework Normalizes Completions Tags** — Molly Smith, Sarah Carr and Huzeifa Ismail, Murphy Exploration and Production, 28 January 2021 | A strong industrial example of string matching and human review. In a historian with more than a million completions tags, Murphy identified **3,526 distinct name variants** and reduced them to **58 standard tags**. The workflow used edit-distance matching, retained the native name, and sent unfamiliar variants to a person whose decision was saved for reuse. The paper gives counts but no held-out accuracy percentage. [Operator-authored article](https://jpt.spe.org/proposed-framework-normalizes-completions-tags) |
| **Sequential Learning with Active Partial Labeling for Building Metadata** — Lu Lin, Zheng Luo, Dezhi Hong and Hongning Wang, ACM BuildSys, 13–14 November 2019 | The model asked people to label selected pieces of building point names. With 15 labeled names initially, it reached **94%** on EBU3B after 650 additional character labels, and **95%** on SDH and **90%** on IBM after 600 each. These are **phrase-level** accuracies under eight-fold cross-validation. A phrase counted only when every character in it was labeled correctly. [Authors’ paper, §§4.1–4.2](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~dehong/pdf/buildsys19b.pdf) |
| **Advancing smart building readiness: Automated metadata extraction using neural language processing methods** — David Waterworth, Subbu Sethuvenkatraman and Quan Z. Sheng, online 25 May 2021 | A language model learned building-specific abbreviations from 152 buildings and was evaluated on a further 30. The conclusion reports **71% accuracy**. The authors define their accuracy measure as Jaccard similarity, the overlap between predicted and reference tag sets. A partially correct set therefore earns partial credit. [Published paper, §§4.2 and 6](https://research-management.mq.edu.au/ws/portalfiles/portal/187387120/187386043.pdf) |
| **Automated Tagging and Semantic Mapping of BACnet Metadata to Brick Schema for Building Automation Systems** — Milan Jain, Robert Lutes and Srinivas Katipamula, Pacific Northwest National Laboratory, report dated September 2025, posted 28 October 2026 | Across **7,429 points in eight buildings**, the pipeline used keyword retrieval, sentence embeddings and expert mappings. Table 4’s counts support **28.3%** for the best conventional classifier, **26.6%** for Llama3 and **35.6%** for Llama3 with retrieved examples. **The report is internally inconsistent.** Its PDF abstract gives **22.0%, 24.1% and 27.9%**, respectively. The results section and laboratory publication page use the first set. [Report, abstract and table 4](https://www.pnnl.gov/main/publications/external/technical_reports/PNNL-38511.pdf) · [PNNL publication record](https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/automated-tagging-and-semantic-mapping-bacnet-metadata-brick-schema-building) |
| **Point2Brick: Automating building semantic model creation via multi-stage confidence-aware Large Language Model agents** — Zicheng Wang, Jie Ma, Jie Cai and Ming Qu, *Energy and Buildings*, 2026 | Agents interpret the point name, retrieve plausible Brick concepts, validate the class and revisit low-confidence cases. The authors report **78.4%** overall accuracy on curated public and proprietary building data. On the challenging subset, refinement improved accuracy from **33.3% to 59.3%**. The target was the exact Brick class. I verified these figures in the publisher’s abstract and preview, but could not verify the subset size. [Publisher’s paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enbuild.2026.117516) |
| **A Transferable Retrieval-and-Reranking Framework for Mapping BMS Points to Building Semantic Metadata** — Ke Sun et al., SSRN preprint posted 12 August 2026 | On **1,000 points from 12 held-out buildings covering 25 Brick classes**, the authors report **37.30%** accuracy for a direct classifier, **80.50%** after semantic reranking and **82.80%** using the annual time-series branch. Combining evidence for each candidate reached **96.70% accuracy** and **79.49% macro-F1**, which gives each class equal weight. This is evidence that the signal’s behaviour can help resolve an ambiguous name. The figures are from the authors’ preprint abstract. [SSRN preprint](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7274155) |
| **LLMs for industrial databases: an agro-food production plant use case** — Lacramioara Dranca et al., *Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence*, 29 May 2026 | The closest study I found to the proposed on-site agent. It embedded descriptions of actual plant database fields using BGE-M3, ranked them by cosine similarity and followed their parent relationships to build a small schema for the model. The authors tested **56 Spanish questions**. Even when CodeGemma-2B received the **correct** fields, its Flux key-lookup queries had an execution-success fraction of **0.875** and an exact-result-match fraction of **0.083 ± 0.072**. For a mine trial, this argues for scoring two things separately: whether the agent selected the right point, and whether it returned the right value for the requested time. [Paper, §§3.2.3 and 4.3, table 11](https://doi.org/10.3389/frai.2026.1764367) |
